The long letter · six chapters

Chapter one

I don't really know how to write a letter to you, because you already know everything I'd say.

But it's your birthday, and some things are worth saying properly, in full sentences, without a screen between us. So here it is — unedited, the way six years actually happened, not the highlight reel version.

The long letterChapter two

Chapter two

The one you know I hate

Istill remember the phase when you went from my chill, carefree girl to “I think I'm falling in love” girl. And of course, it had to be that one. You know I hate him (P).

I told you not to talk to him approximately one hundred times, probably more, and every single time you somehow found a reason to talk to him again.

I watched you go from excited and completely in love to confused, hurt and crying. The arguments, the misunderstandings, the breaking up, the getting back together, and all the “this is the last time” and कसम बोल्न छाडिसके conversations.

And honestly, I hated seeing you hurt. There were so many moments when I wanted to shake you and say, छोड्दे यार प्लिज भयो अब — but I never did, because some things you have to learn on your own, no matter how many times your best friend says it.

But there were also moments when I just sat there and listened, because sometimes you don't need someone to say I told you so. You just need your person to stay.

Chapter three

The trips that made it

Every one of those trips started as a bad idea that neither of us tried very hard to talk the other out of. Chitwan for no reason. Kulekhani because why not. A trek we were not dressed for, up a hill neither of us trained for.

And somehow those are the memories that come back first — not the plans that went right, the ones that went sideways and became stories we still tell wrong on purpose.

The long letterChapter four

Chapter four

My birthday reminder to you

Read this part twice

If someone had told me in 2020 that the random girl I met in college would become one of the most important people in my life, I probably wouldn't have believed them. Because look at us now.

From seven people in a college group to somehow becoming just the two of us who are still here. From random café plans to actual trips. From talking about boys to talking about careers, money, future, life and everything in between.

From being two stupid college girls to slowly becoming adults who are still figuring everything out. We've grown up together, and maybe that's why this friendship feels so different.

You know the versions of me that most people don't. And I know the versions of you that most people don't. And somehow, after seeing all of it, we still love each other endlessly.

On your birthday, I don't just want to remind you how much I love you. I want to remind you of who you are. You're stronger than you think. You're capable of so much more than you give yourself credit for. You've grown so much since the girl I met in 2020, and I'm genuinely proud of the person you're becoming.

Chapter six · closing

Wherever life takes us

I don't know what the next six years look like. Neither do you. Careers, cities, people, none of it is promised to stay the same.

But I know that whatever changes, I want you in it. Not as a photo from 2020, as an actual person I still call first.

So happy birthday, मेरो पुन्टु. Because no matter where life takes us, I hope it always brings us back to each other.
